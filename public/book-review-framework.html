📚 Book Review Framework

A Progressive Guide for Elementary Students — Grades 1 through 5

Beginner Grade 1–2
Section Prompt Guiding Questions Response
Title & Author "The book is called ___. It was written by ___."
  • What is the name of the book?
  • Who wrote it?
  • Who drew the pictures?
 1 sentence
Characters "The main character is ___."
  • Who is the story about?
  • Is it a person, an animal, or something else?
 1 sentence
What Happened? "The story is about ___."
  • What did the character do?
  • What happened first?
  • What happened at the end?
 1–2 sentences
Favorite Part "My favorite part is when ___."
  • What part made you laugh?
  • What part made you feel happy or sad?
  • What picture did you like most?
 1 sentence
Rating Draw 1–5 stars
  • Did you love it (5 stars) or not like it (1 star)?
  • Would you read it again?
 Stars only
Recommendation "I think my friend would / would not like this book because ___."
  • Who would enjoy this book?
  • Would your best friend like it?
  • Why or why not?
 1 sentence
Intermediate Grade 3
Section Prompt Guiding Questions Response
Title, Author & Genre Title, author, fiction / nonfiction
  • Is this a real story or a made-up story?
  • Is it funny, scary, or adventurous?
 1–2 sentences
Setting "The story takes place in ___."
  • Where does the story happen?
  • When does it happen — long ago, now, or in the future?
  • Does the setting change?
 1 sentence
Characters Describe the main character
  • What does the character look like?
  • How does the character act?
  • Is the character kind, brave, shy, or funny?
 2–3 sentences
Summary (B-M-E) Beginning / Middle / End
  • What happened at the start?
  • What big event happened in the middle?
  • How did the story end?
 3–4 sentences
Problem & Solution "The problem is ___. It was solved by ___."
  • What trouble did the character face?
  • How did they try to fix it?
  • Did someone help them?
  • Did the solution work?
 2 sentences
Opinion "I liked / didn't like this book because ___."
  • What made this book good or boring?
  • Was it easy or hard to read?
  • Did anything surprise you?
 2 sentences
Rating 1–5 stars with one reason
  • How does this compare to other books you've read?
  • What is one reason for your rating?
 1 sentence + stars
Upper Intermediate Grade 4
Section Prompt Guiding Questions Response
Introduction Title, author, genre, and a hook sentence
  • What type of book is this?
  • What makes it interesting enough to review?
  • Can you start with a question or exciting fact?
 2–3 sentences
Setting & Mood Where/when + how it makes the reader feel
  • Does the setting feel safe, scary, magical, or realistic?
  • How does the author describe it?
  • Does the setting change the mood of the story?
 2 sentences
Characters Main & supporting characters, traits, growth
  • Who are the most important characters?
  • How do they change from the beginning to the end?
  • What challenges shape them?
  • Who helps or blocks the main character?
 3–4 sentences
Plot Summary Beginning, conflict, climax, resolution
  • What starts the action?
  • What is the biggest conflict?
  • What is the turning point?
  • How is the conflict resolved? (Don't give away the ending!)
 4–5 sentences
Theme / Lesson "The author's message is ___."
  • What lesson does the character learn?
  • What is the author trying to teach readers?
  • Can you state the message in one sentence?
 1–2 sentences
Personal Response Connect to own life or another book
  • Have you ever felt like the main character?
  • Does this remind you of another book or a real experience?
  • How did the book change how you think about something?
 2–3 sentences
Recommendation Who should read it and why
  • What age group would enjoy this?
  • Would someone who likes adventure / humor / mystery enjoy this?
 2 sentences
Rating 1–5 stars with supporting reasons
  • What did the author do well?
  • What could be better?
  • Is your rating about the story, the writing, or both?
 1–2 sentences
Advanced Grade 5
Section Prompt Guiding Questions Response
Introduction Title, author, genre, thesis statement
  • What is your overall opinion in one clear sentence (thesis)?
  • What background does the reader need?
  • Can you open with a hook — a quote, a question, or a bold statement?
 3–4 sentences
Setting & Atmosphere Time, place, effect on plot
  • How does the setting influence the characters' choices?
  • Would the story work in a different time or place?
  • What words does the author use to create atmosphere?
 2–3 sentences
Character Analysis Traits, motivations, development, relationships
  • What drives the main character's actions?
  • How are they different at the end vs. the beginning?
  • What internal conflicts do they face?
  • How do relationships between characters create tension or support?
 4–5 sentences
Plot Summary Full story structure (spoiler-free)
  • What is the inciting incident?
  • How does tension build?
  • What is the climax?
  • How are loose ends tied up?
  • Can you summarize without spoiling the ending?
 5–6 sentences
Theme & Author's Purpose Central theme + why the author wrote it
  • What big idea does the story explore (friendship, justice, identity)?
  • Why did the author choose this topic?
  • Is the theme stated directly or implied?
  • How does the title connect to the theme?
 2–3 sentences
Literary Elements Figurative language, foreshadowing, symbolism, POV
  • Did the author use metaphors, similes, or personification?
  • Are there clues early in the story that hint at later events?
  • Does any object or event represent something deeper?
  • Is the story told in first or third person — and why does that matter?
 2–3 sentences
Critical Opinion Strengths, weaknesses, text evidence
  • What did the author do best — plot, characters, or language?
  • What felt weak or confusing?
  • Can you quote or reference a specific passage to support your opinion?
  • Is the ending satisfying? Why or why not?
 3–4 sentences
Comparison Compare to another book
  • Have you read another book by the same author — how do they compare?
  • Does this book remind you of another in the same genre?
  • What makes this one stand out or fall short?
 2 sentences
Recommendation & Rating Target audience + 1–5 stars + justification
  • Who is the ideal reader for this book?
  • What reading level is it best for?
  • Can you give one strong reason for your star rating?
  • Would you read a sequel?
 2–3 sentences
Quick Comparison Across Grades
Skill G1–2 G3 G4 G5
Length 5–7 sentences 10–15 sentences 1 page 1–1.5 pages
Summary Style "It's about…" B-M-E Plot arc (no spoilers) Full story structure
Character Depth Name only Traits Traits + growth Analysis + motivation
Opinion Type Like / don't like With 1 reason With life connection With text evidence
Theme — Simple lesson Author's message Theme + author's purpose
Literary Elements — — — Figurative language, POV, symbolism
Question Complexity What? Who? How? Why? (simple) How? Why? (analytical) Why? What if? (critical)
Comparison — — — Cross-text comparison
Vocabulary Focus Sight words Adjectives Transition words Academic language
Progression Summary:
G1–2: Literal recall (What? Who?) → G3: Inferential thinking (How? Why?) → G4: Analytical response (How? Why? + connections) → G5: Critical evaluation (Why does it matter? What evidence supports your view?)
